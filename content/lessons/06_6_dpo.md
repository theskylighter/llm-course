**Direct Preference Optimization reaches the same optimum as KL-regularized RLHF in closed form, so it trains on preference pairs with a simple loss and needs neither a separate reward model nor an RL loop.** The reward is implicit in the log-ratio of policy to reference.

> [!compiler] Source note
> The repo has no lecture for DPO (Lecture 24 only names it under "contrastive learning" as "an advanced version of PPO"). This lesson is derived from MidSem Q7.

## The Problem: PPO Is Heavy and Unstable

PPO needs four models in memory, sampling during training, and a separate reward-model stage. Could we skip them?

## The Derivation

**Step 1.** The objective $\max_\pi\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi}[r-\beta\log\frac{\pi}{\pi_{ref}}]$ has the optimum

$$\pi^*(y|x)=\frac{1}{Z(x)}\pi_{ref}(y|x)\,e^{r(x,y)/\beta},\qquad Z(x)=\sum_y\pi_{ref}\,e^{r/\beta}.$$

**Step 2.** Invert: $r(x,y)=\beta\log\frac{\pi^*(y|x)}{\pi_{ref}(y|x)}+\beta\log Z(x)$.

**Step 3.** Put this into Bradley-Terry (lesson 6.4). The intractable $\beta\log Z(x)$ appears in both rewards and **cancels** in the difference. Replacing $\pi^*$ by the trainable $\pi_\theta$:

$$\mathcal{L}_{DPO}=-\mathbb{E}_{(x,y^+,y^-)}\log\sigma\!\Big(\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y^+|x)}{\pi_{ref}(y^+|x)}-\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y^-|x)}{\pi_{ref}(y^-|x)}\Big)$$

**Gradient** with $\hat r=\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta}{\pi_{ref}}$:

$$\nabla_\theta\mathcal{L}_{DPO}=-\beta\,\mathbb{E}\big[\sigma(\hat r(y^-)-\hat r(y^+))\,(\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(y^+|x)-\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(y^-|x))\big]$$

The bracket raises the likelihood of $y^+$ and lowers that of $y^-$, and the weight is largest when the implicit reward **mis-orders** the pair.

:::analogy
Instead of hiring a judge to score contestants and then training them (PPO), DPO shows the contestant pairs of "better" and "worse" performances and nudges the contestant directly, comparing against their starting level (the reference).
:::

```python
import math
sigma = lambda z: 1 / (1 + math.exp(-z))
beta = 0.1

def dpo_loss(lp_pos, lp_neg, ref_pos, ref_neg):
    margin = beta * (lp_pos - ref_pos) - beta * (lp_neg - ref_neg)
    return -math.log(sigma(margin)), sigma(-margin)      # loss, gradient weight

print("at the reference (policy = ref):", [round(v, 3) for v in dpo_loss(-10, -12, -10, -12)])
print("policy prefers y+ more:        ", [round(v, 3) for v in dpo_loss(-8, -15, -10, -12)])
print("policy mis-orders the pair:    ", [round(v, 3) for v in dpo_loss(-14, -9, -10, -12)])
```

```output
at the reference (policy = ref): [0.693, 0.5]
policy prefers y+ more:         [0.474, 0.378]
policy mis-orders the pair:     [1.103, 0.668]
```

| | PPO-RLHF | DPO |
|---|---|---|
| Online / offline | online (samples from the policy) | offline (fixed preference pairs) |
| Reward model | explicit, trained first | implicit, $\beta\log\pi_\theta/\pi_{ref}$ |
| Models in memory | policy, reference, reward, value | policy, reference |
| Taxonomy (Lec 24, 13.1) | reward maximization | contrastive learning |

> [!compiler] Alignment tax (from memory of InstructGPT, verify)
> Optimizing a learned reward can lower performance on ordinary NLP benchmarks relative to the base model, the **alignment tax**. InstructGPT reduces it by mixing in pre-training gradients during PPO ("PPO-ptx"). The KL term exists to keep the policy near $\pi_{ref}$; reward hacking is the failure when it is too weak.

:::def
**DPO** minimises $-\log\sigma\!\big(\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y^+|x)}{\pi_{ref}(y^+|x)}-\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y^-|x)}{\pi_{ref}(y^-|x)}\big)$ over preference pairs, which is equivalent to optimising the KL-regularized RLHF objective with an implicit reward.
:::

:::key
- Closed-form optimum $\pi^*\propto\pi_{ref}e^{r/\beta}$; invert it to get an implicit reward.
- $\beta\log Z(x)$ cancels in the Bradley-Terry difference.
- Offline, two models (policy and reference), no sampling, no value head.
:::

:::mistakes
- (EndSem Q2(c)) Preference-tuning a base model **without** SFT: the reference gives tiny probability to preferred chat-format outputs, so the log-ratios are poorly behaved; do SFT first.
:::

:::exam
- *(MidSem Q7(a))* PPO versus DPO: PPO is online RL with an explicit reward model and sampling; DPO optimises preference pairs directly with an implicit reward.
- *(EndSem Q2(b))* Adding $h(x)$ to the reward leaves BT probabilities and the optimal policy unchanged, since it appears in both numerator and $Z(x)$.
:::

## Quick Check

:::q Q1 · Why does $Z(x)$ not appear in the DPO loss?
:::ans
It enters both rewards in the Bradley-Terry difference and cancels.
:::
:::

:::q Q2 · When is the DPO gradient weight largest?
:::ans
When the implicit reward mis-orders the pair, that is when it ranks $y^-$ above $y^+$.
:::
