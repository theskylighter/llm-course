**Rotary positional encoding rotates query and key vectors by an angle proportional to position, so their dot product depends only on the relative offset.** It is multiplicative, has nothing to learn, and is used in LLaMA.

## The Problem: Absolute Positions Hide Relative Offsets

> [!lecture] Absolute versus relative
> **Absolute:** encode where a token *is* (learned table, or sinusoid). **Relative:** we don't care about the absolute position, only about the offset between a pair. *The dog chased the pig* and *once upon a time the pig chased the dog*: the words have shifted and swapped, but offsets are what matter.

> [!slides] Relative position embeddings (before RoPE)
> - **Shaw et al.:** trainable relative embeddings added to *keys and values only*, with $r=\operatorname{clip}(m-n,\,r_{\min},r_{\max})$.
> - **Transformer-XL:** expand $(W_q(x_m+p_m))^{\top}(W_k(x_n+p_n))$ into four terms and replace $p_n$ by $\tilde p_{m-n}$.
> - **T5:** learn a bias $b_{i,j}$ per relative offset, added to the $QK^{\top}$ score.
> - **DeBERTa:** keep a selected subset of the terms.

## The Idea: Rotation

> [!lecture] The idea
> *The dog chased the pig* and *the pig chased the dog*: "dog" and "pig" are always two words apart. We capture that with the **angle between their two vectors**. If both words shift in absolute position, the two vectors **rotate together**, so the angle between them does not change, but the rotation itself carries the absolute position. Beautiful idea, right? RoPE is the first method that uses absolute and relative information together. It is adopted by PaLM, GPT-Neo and GPT-J, and LLaMA. Everything else in the Transformer stays the same.

> [!slides] RoPE
> **Requirement.** Find $f_q,f_k,g$ such that $\langle f_q(\mathbf{x}_m,m),\,f_k(\mathbf{x}_n,n)\rangle=g(\mathbf{x}_m,\mathbf{x}_n,\,m-n)$.
> **Solution for $d=2$:** $f_q=(W_q\mathbf{x}_m)\,e^{im\theta}$, $f_k=(W_k\mathbf{x}_n)\,e^{in\theta}$, in matrix form the rotation $R_{m\theta}=\begin{bmatrix}\cos m\theta&-\sin m\theta\\\sin m\theta&\cos m\theta\end{bmatrix}$.
> **General $d$:** split the vector into $d/2$ two-dimensional sub-spaces and rotate each pair by its own angle $\theta_i=10000^{-2(i-1)/d}$. **There is nothing to learn.**

> [!compiler] Worked derivation: why the relative position falls out
> $$(R_{m\theta}\mathbf{q})^{\top}(R_{n\theta}\mathbf{k})=\mathbf{q}^{\top}R_{m\theta}^{\top}R_{n\theta}\,\mathbf{k}=\mathbf{q}^{\top}R_{(n-m)\theta}\,\mathbf{k},$$
> because $R_a^{\top}=R_{-a}$ and rotations compose by adding angles. Only $n-m$ survives. Rotations are orthogonal, so $\|R\mathbf{x}\|=\|\mathbf{x}\|$. In the PyTorch code you apply the rotation to $Q$ and $K$ **inside each attention layer**, *not* by adding anything to the input embeddings.

:::analogy
Two clock hands pointing at the same time. If both are turned by the same amount, the angle between them is unchanged; only turning them by different amounts changes it.
:::

```python
import numpy as np
def rot(theta):
    return np.array([[np.cos(theta), -np.sin(theta)], [np.sin(theta), np.cos(theta)]])

q = np.array([1.0, 0.5]); k = np.array([0.3, -0.8]); th = 0.4
def score(m, n):
    return (rot(m * th) @ q) @ (rot(n * th) @ k)

print("offset 3 at (2,5), (10,13), (100,103):", [round(float(score(m, m + 3)), 6) for m in (2, 10, 100)])
print("offset 4 at (2,6):", round(float(score(2, 6)), 6))
print("norm preserved:", np.isclose(np.linalg.norm(rot(2.2) @ q), np.linalg.norm(q)))
```

```output
offset 3 at (2,5), (10,13), (100,103): [0.849201, 0.849201, 0.849201]
offset 4 at (2,6): 0.952515
norm preserved: True
```

> [!compiler] Date check
> The lecture says RoPE "was uploaded in 2023". The RoFormer paper first appeared in **April 2021**. The lecture's claim that "nobody uses the sinusoidal representation" is an overstatement for *new LLMs*: it is accurate for the LLaMA family, but learned tables (BERT, GPT-2) are still in use.

:::def
**Rotary positional encoding (RoPE)** rotates each 2-D pair of the query and key vectors at position $m$ by angle $m\theta_i$, so that $\langle R_{m\theta}\mathbf{q},R_{n\theta}\mathbf{k}\rangle$ depends on positions only through $n-m$.
:::

:::key
- RoPE is **multiplicative** (a rotation), not additive; applied to $Q$ and $K$ in every attention layer.
- The score depends on relative offset only; nothing is learned.
- Rotations preserve norms; the inner product decays with relative distance.
:::

:::mistakes
- (EndSem 2024 Q0(c)) *"RoPE represents positions as complex numbers and tokens as pure rotations"* is **false**: tokens (the $q$, $k$ vectors) are the complex numbers and positions are the rotations.
- "RoPE adds a vector to the embedding." It does not.
:::

- *True/False with justification (2 marks):* the RoPE statement above.

## Quick Check

:::q Q1 · Why does the RoPE dot product depend only on the relative offset?
:::ans
$R_{m\theta}^\top R_{n\theta}=R_{(n-m)\theta}$, so shifting both positions by the same amount leaves the product unchanged.
:::
